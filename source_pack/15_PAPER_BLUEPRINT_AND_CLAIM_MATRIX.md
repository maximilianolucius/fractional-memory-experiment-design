10. Simulation benchmark

13. Conclusion
14. Proof appendices

## Claim matrix

### Claims permitted after analytical work alone

- the exact model formulas and baseline stability boundary;
- structural non-equivalence under the direct-channel assumptions;
- finite-horizon approximation and impossibility statements;
- optimality theorems for fixed linear-Gaussian models;
- safety existence and sufficient barrier conditions.

### Claims requiring simulation artifacts

- which waveform wins under a specified budget;
- confusion rates among model classes;
- recovery/coverage of \(\alpha\), \(\tau\), or latent rates;
- robustness to noise and priors;
- computational cost.

### Claims requiring real experiments

- ecological evidence for genuine fractional memory;
- laboratory feasibility and welfare/safety of interventions;
- external validity beyond the calibrated system;
- biological interpretation of \(\alpha\).

## Claims that must not appear

- “a fitted \(\alpha<1\) proves biological memory”;
- “a single gamma delay converges to a power law by setting its rate to zero”;
- “fractional nonlinear systems become ODEs after \(t\mapsto t^\alpha\)”;
- “distributed-order stability depends only on mean order”;
- “component-specific orders use independent Matignon wedges”;
- “a Matignon crossing creates a limit cycle” for the standard autonomous Caputo model;
- any empirical number not generated by the released pipeline.

## Minimum publishable contribution

A publishable first version does not require a physical experiment if it contains:

1. the exact theorem set;
2. a restricted and transparent model family;
3. a validated nonlinear simulation benchmark;
4. reproducible code and stored design/results artifacts;
5. an implementable prospective laboratory protocol.

## Strongest paper narrative

1. Ideal broadband data separate fractional from finite latent memory.
2. Finite horizon and noise allow latent approximation.
3. Unrestricted latent dimension makes the question ill-posed.
4. Complexity restrictions restore a meaningful design problem.
5. Optimal excitation concentrates on a small number of frequencies/channels.
6. Safety and the Allee threshold constrain which theoretical optimum can be implemented.
