\section{Introduction and Falsifiable Memory Question}
\label{sec:introduction}

The problem of distinguishing fractional, delayed, and latent ecological memory effects in dynamical systems is fundamental to understanding ecosystem dynamics under perturbation. Fractional derivatives model hereditary and nonlocal dynamics naturally~\cite{F01,F02,F03,F04}, and fractional predator--prey systems form an established literature~\cite{E06,E07,E08,E09}.
Experiment design for fractional-order identification already exists~\cite{FOED01,FOED02,FOED03,FOED04}, and ecological perturbation experiments are feasible in laboratory microcosms~\cite{E10,E11,E12}. Safety-constrained information acquisition and safe active model discrimination are also established general problems~\cite{SAFE26A,SAFE26B}. Our novelty claim is therefore deliberately narrower: we study how finite-horizon approximation of fractional memory by bounded-complexity latent responses interacts with a strong-Allee ecological safety barrier and a declared experimental protocol.

The finite-horizon problem is meaningful only after the rival complexity is controlled. We impose a finite latent-state budget $m$. Theorem~\ref{thm:T10} shows, in its stated linearized fixed-input scope, that increasing latent complexity can drive the finite-horizon output discrepancy below any prescribed tolerance; without a complexity restriction, a fixed finite experiment cannot provide a uniform separation guarantee against the entire latent hierarchy.

Our approach centers on a certified predator--prey model with a strong Allee effect~\cite{E03,E04,E05,P01}, which supplies the ecological operating point and safety geometry. We compare four candidate memory mechanisms at a common experimental reference:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Fractional memory (Caputo derivative of order $\alpha$ --- the only mechanism carrying a fractional order),
    \item Integer-order memory (ODE),
    \item Delayed memory (a strictly proper retarded single-discrete-delay class in the scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:T5}),
    \item Latent memory (finite-dimensional linear time-invariant response models of dimension $m$).
\end{itemize}
The ODE and Caputo mechanisms share the ecological Jacobian $J(A)$ exactly. The DDE and latent alternatives are calibrated rival response laws compared at the same operating point through matched input/observation channels and physical units; their internal generators are not assumed to equal $J(A)$.

The structural part separates the Caputo signature from finite-dimensional rational latent responses~\cite{I05} and from the declared strictly proper retarded single-delay class~\cite{D01,D02}. Theorems~\ref{thm:T4} and~\ref{thm:T5} state the exact scope; no claim is made against every possible delay realization.

Beyond structural separation, we establish a quantitative testing obstruction for a bounded Gaussian observation experiment. Theorems~\ref{thm:T20}--\ref{thm:T22} connect finite-horizon kernel approximation, latent complexity, and a protocol-restricted Strong-Allee safety budget at the kernel level. Theorem~\ref{thm:T21} supplies the matching scope limitation: a one-sided state threshold alone does not control input energy without a physical experiment class. We then work directly with the exact prey-to-prey Caputo transfer, rather than the invalid factorization $(s^\alpha I-J)^{-1}=(sI-J)^{-1}s^{-\alpha}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:polebranch} separates its principal-sheet pole pair from the branch-cut remainder; Theorem~\ref{thm:T23} gives interval-certified finite-state approximation enclosures for this exact prey response, and Corollary~\ref{cor:state_testing} converts them into testing lower bounds under a declared actuator/shape budget. This closes the response-level approximation/testing gap for the linearized prey channel, while a physically constrained hierarchy-wide Strong-Allee safety theorem at full-state level remains open.

Furthermore, we leverage the finite-horizon exponential approximation of the fractional kernel (Theorem~\ref{thm:T9b}) and the associated $L^1$ error bound to construct optimal excitation signals that exploit the frequency-band separation between the fractional and approximate integer-order dynamics.
The finite-horizon impossibility obstacle (Theorem~\ref{thm:T10}, in its linearized fixed-input scope) shows that sufficiently rich latent approximations can drive the discrepancy below any fixed observational resolution on a finite horizon, which motivates an explicit finite-dimensional complexity budget.

Finally, we formulate the optimal experimental design problem under energy and safety constraints, leveraging the principal-singular-vector solution for pairwise optimal inputs (Theorem~\ref{thm:T11}) and the finite-support property of the optimal frequency-domain design (Theorem~\ref{thm:T12}).
The resulting experimental protocol is validated through a reproducible simulation benchmark (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}) and complemented by a prospective microcosm protocol for ecological validation (Section~\ref{sec:prospect}).
