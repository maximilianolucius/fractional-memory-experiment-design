\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Caputo derivatives of order $0<\alpha<1$ model hereditary effects in viscoelasticity, anomalous transport, and population dynamics~\cite{F01,F02,F03,F04}; fractional predator--prey models in particular form a sizeable literature~\cite{E06,E07,E08,E09}. An empirical difficulty sits underneath all of it. Estimating $\alpha<1$ from data does not establish a fractional mechanism, because on a bounded window $[0,T]$ a delay term $\xi(t-\tau)$ or a low-dimensional latent state $q\in\mathbb{R}^m$ generates trajectories within the noise floor of the fractional ones (Theorem~\ref{thm:T9b} makes this quantitative). Design methods for fractional-order \emph{estimation} exist~\cite{FOED01,FOED02,FOED03,FOED04}; safety-constrained information acquisition has been analyzed in general settings~\cite{SAFE26A,SAFE26B}. The intersection --- discrimination between memory \emph{mechanisms}, under an ecological safety constraint --- is the subject here:

\begin{quote}
\emph{Which perturbations and measurements can distinguish fractional memory from delayed or latent alternatives in a strong-Allee ecological system, and when does ecological safety make that discrimination fundamentally difficult?}
\end{quote}

Safety is not decorative in this question. Below the Allee threshold $A$ the prey growth term $P(x)=rx(1-x/K)(x/A-1)$ turns negative and collapse is irreversible, so the perturbations most informative about memory ($\|u\|_\infty$ near the budget, broadband spectra) are the ones most likely to destroy the object of study. Section~\ref{sec:benchmark} measures this numerically: the best-discriminating input crosses the threshold in $100\%$ of stable-regime cells.

The ecological backbone is not built here. The equations, the locked parameters $(r,K,a,h,e,m)=(\tfrac32,1,1,\tfrac12,\tfrac45,\tfrac25)$, the equilibrium $x^*=2/3$, $y^*(A)=2(2-3A)/(9A)$, and the Jacobian $J(A)$ all come from a companion study~\cite{E03,E04,E05,P01} where they were certified by validated numerics. Reuse is deliberate: with the ecology frozen, the discrimination question can be isolated from any change in biological mechanism. Nothing from~\cite{P01} is claimed as new. The new objects are the four rival response laws (Section~\ref{sec:model}), the perturbation/observation architecture, the finite-horizon discrimination bounds of Section~\ref{sec:analysis}, the combined fractional-delay model~\eqref{eq:fdmodel}, and the benchmark of Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}. Only the ODE and Caputo mechanisms share $J(A)$ exactly; the delay and latent rivals are calibrated alternative response laws matched at the same operating point, input channel, and units.

Exactly four ingredients are inherited from~\cite{P01}: the strong-Allee equations, the locked parameters, the coexistence equilibrium, and $J(A)$. The methodological separation matters as well. A stability certificate decides whether a specified Caputo model is consistent with a dynamical regime; it does not decide whether that model can be told apart from a delay or latent mechanism reproducing the same observations to within $\sigma$. Conversely, no result below recertifies the backbone. We condition on the certified operating geometry of~\cite{P01} and ask how much perturbation and measurement information the discrimination task needs on top of it.

\paragraph{Main contributions.}
Four contributions organize the paper.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Structural and finite-horizon separation} (Theorems~\ref{thm:T4} and~\ref{thm:T9b}). The collocated Caputo transfer decays as $(CB)\tau_0^{1-\alpha}s^{-\alpha}+O(|s|^{-2\alpha})$, a noninteger signature unreachable by finite rational latent transfers or by the declared strictly proper retarded class ($O(\omega^{-1})$ decay). Simultaneously, positive exponential mixtures with $m$ modes reach any prescribed $L^1(0,T)$ tolerance, so separation is meaningful only relative to a declared budget $m$.
    \item \textbf{Certified prey-response testing obstruction} (Theorems~\ref{thm:T20} and~\ref{thm:T23}). On the linearized prey channel, a pole/branch decomposition yields finite-state surrogates with interval-enclosed $L^1$ errors ($0.5335$ at $m=4$ down to $0.0070$ at $m=32$; $\alpha=0.85$, $A=0.25$, $T=12$). Under the pulse budget $\|u\|_2\le0.120$ and $\sigma=0.10$, the Pinsker minimax bound rises from $0.340$ to $0.498$ --- to chance, effectively.
    \item \textbf{Fractional-delay stress test} (Section~\ref{sec:fd}). The combined model~\eqref{eq:fdmodel} equals the pure Caputo system at $\tau=0$ and the DDE at $\alpha=1$; simulations over $(\alpha,\tau)\in[0.70,1]\times[0,0.5]$ show delay shifting the predator peak by up to $\approx0.7$ time units, eroding the minimum Allee margin to $0.238$ at $\alpha=0.95$, and generating interior responses far from both limits (Table~\ref{tab:fd-bridge-cells}).
    \item \textbf{Safety--informativeness trade-off} (Tables~\ref{tab:benchmark-safety} and~\ref{tab:fd-design-ranking}). Across six input families: PRBS and multisine discriminate best ($0.584$, $0.583$ macro-accuracy) with Allee-crossing rates of $1.000$ and $0.357$; multiscale never crosses and scores $0.324$. The constrained design problem is measured, not merely postulated.
\end{enumerate}

Complete proofs, secondary results, the Bayesian sequential extension, and solver derivations are in the Supplementary Material; the main text keeps the four statements needed to read the numerics.
