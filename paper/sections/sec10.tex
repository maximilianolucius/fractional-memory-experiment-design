\section{Calibrated, Safety-Instrumented Simulation Benchmark}
\label{sec:benchmark}

All numbers below are generated by the released code (\texttt{benchmark/}) and result
files (\texttt{benchmark/results/}); none are entered by hand. The full-factorial
sweep was executed on a 344-core server (300 workers).

\subsection{Rival calibration and common input budget}
The four mechanisms (ODE, Caputo, retarded-delay DDE, and finite latent-state) are compared at
a common experimental operating point with matched input/observation channels and physical
units. The ODE and Caputo mechanisms share the ecological Jacobian exactly; the DDE and latent
alternatives are calibrated rival response laws and are not assumed to have the same internal
generator. Rivals are chosen to be genuine but subtle: the delay is $\tau=0.35$ in the calibrated
stable regime and the latent coupling is $0.15$. Inputs are compared at a common
\emph{peak-amplitude} budget $\|u\|_\infty\le 0.10$; this is an actuator constraint and does
not by itself certify nonlinear state safety. Two tasks are reported separately, because the integer-order
trace $T(A)=(7A-2)/(8A)$ changes sign at $A=2/7$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Task A (memory-shape), stable regime} $A\in\{0.20,0.25\}$ ($A<2/7$, all rivals
  locally stable): the primary scientific task.
  \item \textbf{Task B (stability verdict)}, $A\in\{0.30,0.40\}$ ($A>2/7$): an easier
  stress-test, reported separately and \emph{not} averaged into the headline.
\end{itemize}
The decision rule is Bayesian Information Criterion model selection; this is a
frequentist/information-criterion benchmark, not an implemented Bayesian evidence computation.

\subsection{Exact and validated components}
The analytic gates hold exactly: $x^\ast=2/3$; $\alpha^\ast(0.30)=0.9690122761517083$ to
twelve digits; prey-only and predator-only channels are both full rank. The Caputo PECE solver
agrees with the exact Mittag--Leffler solution to a maximum absolute error of $2.5\times10^{-4}$
and converges under refinement (Supplementary Material, Section~S7). The primary \emph{computational} result is the exact
linear-Gaussian discrimination under the common peak-amplitude budget (without nonlinear state-safety enforcement), whose design ranking by mean minimum
pairwise KL divergence is
\[
\text{prbs} > \text{pulse} > \text{multiscale} > \text{multisine} > \text{sinusoid} > \text{chirp}.
\]

\subsection{Nonlinear confirmation (Task A, stable regime)}

ranking are:

Accuracy is strongly stratified: by fractional order it falls from $0.70$ at $\alpha=0.70$ to
$0.31$ at $\alpha=0.95$ (near the integer-order limit); by SNR it is $0.76$ (high), $0.44$
(medium), $0.27$ (low); by observation channel it is highest for both species ($0.53$), then
prey-only ($0.52$), then predator-only ($0.43$). Task B (stability verdict) reaches macro
accuracy $0.574$ but only detects stability, not memory shape.

\subsection{Safety--informativeness trade-off}
At the common amplitude budget, safety and informativeness are in tension. Safety is reported with explicit numerical quantities per design: the Allee-crossing rate, the minimum Allee margin $\min_t (x(t)-A)$, and the minimum signed distance $\min_t \min\{x(t)-x_L,\; x_U-x(t),\; y(t)-y_L,\; y_U-y(t)\}$ against the benchmark diagnostic rectangle. The rectangle used by the released code is defined around the coexistence equilibrium $(x^*, y^*)$ with $x^*=2/3$ by
\[
x_L = \max(A+0.02,\, 0.5\,x^*),\qquad x_U = 1.6\,x^*,\qquad
y_L = \max(10^{-3},\, 0.4\,y^*),\qquad y_U = 1.9\,y^*,
\]
so the lower prey face is defined to lie at least $0.02$ above the Allee threshold $A$. All margins below are over the stable-regime cells:

\begin{table}[ht]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lcccc}
    \hline
    Design & Allee-cross rate & $\min_t(x-A)$ & $\min_t$ face distance & Task-A accuracy \\
    \hline
    multiscale & 0\%   & $+0.29$ & $+0.21$ & 0.324 \\
    pulse      & 7\%   & $-0.12$ & $-0.20$ & 0.414 \\
    multisine  & 36\%  & $-0.24$ & $-0.32$ & 0.583 \\
    chirp      & 93\%  & $-0.30$ & $-0.38$ & 0.507 \\
    sinusoid   & 100\% & $-0.31$ & $-0.39$ & 0.534 \\
    prbs       & 100\% & $-0.31$ & $-0.51$ & 0.584 \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Numerical safety margins per design (stable task): Allee-crossing rate, minimum Allee margin $\min_t(x(t)-A)$, and minimum distance to the benchmark diagnostic rectangle. Transient designs (multiscale, pulse) have the lowest crossing rates but discriminate weakly; broadband/sustained designs (prbs, multisine, sinusoid) discriminate best yet cross the strong-Allee threshold. The rectangle is a numerical diagnostic region, not a proved invariant set for the full amplitude budget. Safe active discrimination of subtle memory is therefore intrinsically hard.}
\end{table}

Solver non-divergence ($n_{\mathrm{diverged}}=0$ over all cells) is reported separately from these ecological safety quantities: a trajectory can remain numerically finite while crossing the Allee threshold, and non-divergence is neither equivalent to nor a substitute for the margins above.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}
This benchmark establishes (i) exact analytic and solver components, (ii) an exact
linear-Gaussian design ranking, and (iii) a calibrated, safety-instrumented nonlinear
confirmation. It does \emph{not} implement full Bayesian model evidence, expected
information gain, or adaptive design; the decision rule is BIC. The observed
safety--informativeness trade-off motivates, but does not solve, the safety-constrained
optimal-design problem (finding the amplitude-limited excitation that maximizes discrimination
inside a safety set satisfying the strict invariance conditions), which we leave to future work together with the full Bayesian
sequential layer.
