\section{Analytical Backbone: Four Results Used by the Experiments}
\label{sec:analysis}

The numerical study rests on four analytical statements. Complete proofs, the secondary theorems (optimal input design, multisine support, safety invariance, the sequential information identity), and the Bayesian layer are in the Supplementary Material; here each statement appears with the minimum context needed to use it in Sections~\ref{sec:response}--\ref{sec:benchmark}.

\subsection{Result I: structural separation}
On the collocated channel ($CB\neq0$) the Caputo transfer is
\[
G_\alpha(s)=C\bigl(q_\alpha(s)I-J\bigr)^{-1}B,
\qquad q_\alpha(s)=\tau_0^{-1}(s\tau_0)^\alpha,
\]
with high-frequency expansion
\[
G_\alpha(s)=(CB)\tau_0^{1-\alpha}s^{-\alpha}+O(|s|^{-2\alpha}).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Structural separation]
\label{thm:T4}
For $0<\alpha<1$ and $CB\neq0$, the Caputo transfer cannot equal any finite-dimensional rational latent transfer on a nonempty open subset of a common analytic domain. It also cannot equal a strictly proper finite-state retarded model with finitely many discrete delays, because the latter has $O(\omega^{-1})$ high-frequency decay whereas the Caputo channel has $O(\omega^{-\alpha})$ decay.
\end{theorem}

The $O(\omega^{-\alpha})$ signature is thus exactly unreachable for both declared rival classes. The statement concerns exact equality of transfers on open frequency sets; it makes no promise about experiments with finite bandwidth, horizon $T<\infty$, and noise $\sigma>0$. Theorem~\ref{thm:T9b} covers that regime.

\subsection{Result II: finite-horizon latent approximation}
The Caputo kernel $k_\alpha(t)=t^{\alpha-1}/\Gamma(\alpha)$ admits a diffusive (spectral-mixture) representation; on $[0,T]$ a constructive positive exponential sum $k_m(t)=\sum_{j=1}^m c_je^{-\lambda_jt}$, $c_j\ge0$, achieves
\[
\|k_\alpha-k_m\|_{L^1(0,T)}\le \widehat E_m,
\qquad \widehat E_m\downarrow0
\]
along an expanding nested rate hierarchy.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-horizon approximation and complexity barrier]
\label{thm:T9b}
For every $T<\infty$ and $\varepsilon>0$, there exists a finite positive exponential mixture whose $L^1(0,T)$ distance from the Caputo kernel is below $\varepsilon$. Consequently, without a finite complexity restriction on the latent rival, no fixed finite-horizon experiment can guarantee a uniform positive separation from the entire latent hierarchy.
\end{theorem}

Results I and II fix the problem geometry: exact separation (Theorem~\ref{thm:T4}) coexists with $\varepsilon$-approximation at finite $T$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:T9b}). Discrimination is therefore relative to a declared budget $m$; in the benchmark of Section~\ref{sec:benchmark} the latent class is capped at $m=3$, and in the certified computation of Theorem~\ref{thm:T23} the budget runs over $m\in\{4,8,16,32\}$.

\subsection{Result III: testing obstruction under a physical input budget}
Let $S$ denote the observation operator, $R\succ0$ the Gaussian noise covariance, and
\[
C_{\rm obs}=\tfrac12\|R^{-1/2}S\|^2.
\]
Young's convolution inequality maps the $L^1$ kernel error into an $L^2$ observation-space bound, $\|(k_\alpha-k_m)*u\|_2\le\widehat E_m\|u\|_2$; a two-point Gaussian argument converts that bound into a minimax statement.

\begin{theorem}[Complexity-dependent testing obstruction]
\label{thm:T20}
If the admissible experiment class satisfies $\|u\|_2\le B$, then the composite minimax error for testing the fractional response against a latent hierarchy containing an explicit competitor with error $\widehat E_m$ obeys
\[
P_e^*(m)\ge \Psi\!\left(C_{\rm obs}\widehat E_m^2 B^2\right),
\]
where $\Psi$ is any valid equal-covariance Gaussian two-point lower bound (Pinsker is used in the numerical plots). Thus $P_e^*(m)\to1/2$ whenever $\widehat E_m\to0$ and $B$ remains bounded.
\end{theorem}

Since $\widehat E_m\downarrow0$ while $B$ and $C_{\rm obs}$ stay fixed, $P_e^*(m)\to1/2$: under a capped input budget, a sufficiently rich rival pushes every test toward the equal-prior chance level, independently of the experiment design.

\subsection{Result IV: ecological prey-response approximation}
Results I--III concern the abstract kernel $k_\alpha$. Result IV addresses the measured object itself, the prey-to-prey transfer $g_\alpha$ of the linearization at $(x^*,y^*(A))$. The factorization $(s^\alpha I-J)^{-1}=(sI-J)^{-1}s^{-\alpha}$, though tempting, is false and is not used. Instead $g_\alpha$ is split by contour deformation into its principal-sheet pole pair --- realized \emph{exactly} by a stable two-state integer-order block --- plus a branch-cut remainder, smooth on $(0,\infty)$, which is the only part requiring approximation.

\begin{theorem}[Certified finite-state approximation of the prey response]
\label{thm:T23}
For the displayed Matignon-stable cells and $m\in\{4,8,16,32\}$, there exists an explicit stable finite-state prey-response surrogate $g_m$ such that
\[
\|g_\alpha-g_m\|_{L^1(0,T)}\le \widehat E_m^{\rm state},
\]
where the bound is obtained by outward-rounded interval subdivision and a rigorous tail estimate. At $\alpha=0.85$, $T=12$, the certified enclosure at $A=0.25$ decreases from $0.5335$ ($m=4$) to $0.0070$ ($m=32$).
\end{theorem}

Every displayed constant is an outward-rounded interval enclosure, not a floating-point estimate. The declared rectangular pulse has support length $1.44$, so $\|u\|_\infty\le0.10$ implies $\|u\|_2\le0.10\sqrt{1.44}=0.120$; combining with $\sigma=0.10$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:T20} gives Pinsker bounds $0.340$, $0.462$, $0.492$, $0.498$ at $A=0.25$ for $m=4,8,16,32$ (Table~\ref{tab:state-approx} adds $A=0.30$). At $m=32$ the gap to the chance level $0.500$ is $0.002$: the surrogate is statistically indistinguishable from the fractional response under this protocol.
