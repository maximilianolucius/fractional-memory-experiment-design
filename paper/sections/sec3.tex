\section{Analytical Backbone: Four Results Used by the Experiments}
\label{sec:analysis}

The complete derivations and secondary results are given in the Supplementary Material. Here we retain only the four statements that directly support the numerical study.

\subsection{Result I: structural separation}
For the collocated channel $CB\neq0$, the Caputo transfer
\[
G_\alpha(s)=C\bigl(q_\alpha(s)I-J\bigr)^{-1}B,
\qquad q_\alpha(s)=\tau_0^{-1}(s\tau_0)^\alpha,
\]
has the high-frequency expansion
\[
G_\alpha(s)=(CB)\tau_0^{1-\alpha}s^{-\alpha}+O(|s|^{-2\alpha}).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Structural separation]
\label{thm:T4}
For $0<\alpha<1$ and $CB\neq0$, the Caputo transfer cannot equal any finite-dimensional rational latent transfer on a nonempty open subset of a common analytic domain. It also cannot equal a strictly proper finite-state retarded model with finitely many discrete delays, because the latter has $O(\omega^{-1})$ high-frequency decay whereas the Caputo channel has $O(\omega^{-\alpha})$ decay.
\end{theorem}

The theorem explains why exact discrimination is possible in principle. It does not imply that finite noisy experiments are easy.

\subsection{Result II: finite-horizon latent approximation}
The Caputo memory kernel admits a diffusive representation and can be approximated on $[0,T]$ by a positive exponential sum. For a constructive $m$-mode approximation $k_m$,
\[
\|k_\alpha-k_m\|_{L^1(0,T)}\le \widehat E_m,
\qquad \widehat E_m\downarrow0
\]
along an expanding nested rate hierarchy.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-horizon approximation and complexity barrier]
\label{thm:T9b}
For every $T<\infty$ and $\varepsilon>0$, there exists a finite positive exponential mixture whose $L^1(0,T)$ distance from the Caputo kernel is below $\varepsilon$. Consequently, without a finite complexity restriction on the latent rival, no fixed finite-horizon experiment can guarantee a uniform positive separation from the entire latent hierarchy.
\end{theorem}

This is the analytical reason for treating latent dimension as an experimental-model complexity budget rather than an unconstrained nuisance parameter.

\subsection{Result III: testing obstruction under a physical input budget}
Let $S$ be the observation operator, $R$ the Gaussian covariance, and $C_{\rm obs}=\tfrac12\|R^{-1/2}S\|^2$. Young's inequality converts the approximation bound into an observation-space bound.

\begin{theorem}[Complexity-dependent testing obstruction]
\label{thm:T20}
If the admissible experiment class satisfies $\|u\|_2\le B$, then the composite minimax error for testing the fractional response against a latent hierarchy containing an explicit competitor with error $\widehat E_m$ obeys
\[
P_e^*(m)\ge \Psi\!\left(C_{\rm obs}\widehat E_m^2 B^2\right),
\]
where $\Psi$ is any valid equal-covariance Gaussian two-point lower bound (Pinsker is used in the numerical plots). Thus $P_e^*(m)\to1/2$ whenever $\widehat E_m\to0$ and $B$ remains bounded.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Result IV: ecological prey-response approximation}
The ecological result works with the exact prey-to-prey fractional transfer, avoiding the invalid factorization $(s^\alpha I-J)^{-1}=(sI-J)^{-1}s^{-\alpha}$. A pole/branch decomposition realizes the principal-sheet pole pair exactly with a stable two-state integer-order block and approximates only the branch remainder.

\begin{theorem}[Certified finite-state approximation of the prey response]
\label{thm:T23}
For the displayed Matignon-stable cells and $m\in\{4,8,16,32\}$, there exists an explicit stable finite-state prey-response surrogate $g_m$ such that
\[
\|g_\alpha-g_m\|_{L^1(0,T)}\le \widehat E_m^{\rm state},
\]
where the bound is obtained by outward-rounded interval subdivision and a rigorous tail estimate. At $\alpha=0.85$, $T=12$, the certified enclosure at $A=0.25$ decreases from $0.5335$ ($m=4$) to $0.0070$ ($m=32$).
\end{theorem}

For the declared pulse, $\|u\|_\infty\le0.10$ implies $\|u\|_2\le0.120$. At $\sigma=0.10$, the resulting Pinsker testing lower bounds at $A=0.25$ are approximately $0.340,0.462,0.492,0.498$ for $m=4,8,16,32$, respectively. Hence the finite-state rival becomes statistically indistinguishable from the fractional prey response as its allowed complexity grows.
