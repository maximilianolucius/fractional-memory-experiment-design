\section{Certified strong-Allee ecological backbone}
\label{sec:backbone}

The predator-prey strong-Allee model from the validated literature~\cite{E01,E02,E03,E04,E05,P01} provides a certified ecological nonlinear dynamics that serves as the digital twin for our study.

The model is given by
\begin{align}
    f_1(x,y) &= r x \left(1-\frac{x}{K}\right)\left(\frac{x}{A}-1\right)-\frac{axy}{1+hx}, \\
    f_2(x,y) &= e \frac{axy}{1+hx} - m y,
\end{align}
with locked parameters
\[
r=\frac{3}{2},\quad K=1,\quad a=1,\quad h=\frac{1}{2},\quad e=\frac{4}{5},\quad m=\frac{2}{5}.
\]

The coexistence equilibrium is
\[
x^* = \frac{2}{3}, \qquad y^*(A) = \frac{2(2-3A)}{9A},
\]
which is positive exactly for \(0<A<\frac{2}{3}\).

The Jacobian at coexistence is
\[
J(A) = \begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{7A-2}{8A} & -\dfrac{1}{2} \\[1.2ex]
\dfrac{2-3A}{10A} & 0
\end{pmatrix},
\]
with trace \(T(A)=\frac{7A-2}{8A}\) and determinant \(D(A)=\frac{2-3A}{20A}\).

The discriminant factorizes as
\[
T(A)^2-4D(A) = \frac{(19A-10)(23A-2)}{320A^2}.
\]

For a commensurate Caputo system of order $0<\alpha<1$, the coexistence equilibrium is locally asymptotically stable when every eigenvalue $\mu$ of $J$ satisfies the Matignon sector condition~\cite{F05}:
\[
|\arg\mu| > \frac{\alpha\pi}{2}.
\]

In the regime \(\frac{2}{7}<A<\frac{10}{19}\), the eigenvalues are a complex pair with positive real part. The critical order is
\[
\alpha^*(A) = \frac{2}{\pi} \arctan\left(\frac{\sqrt{4D(A)-T(A)^2}}{T(A)}\right).
\]

Stability holds exactly for \(0<\alpha<\alpha^*(A)\).

At the baseline \(A=0.3\), we have
\[
J=\begin{pmatrix}1/24 & -1/2 \\ 11/30 & 0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
T=\frac{1}{24},\qquad D=\frac{11}{60},
\]
and the exact critical order is
\[
\alpha^*\left(\frac{3}{10}\right) = \frac{2}{\pi}\arctan\sqrt{\frac{2107}{5}} \approx 0.9690122761517084.
\]

Thus $\alpha=0.9$ is stable and $\alpha=1$ is unstable, reproducing the certified verdict~\cite{P01}.

The baseline \(A=0.3\) is exceptionally informative for distinguishing a stable fractional model from the same integer-order skeleton because the models make opposite local stability predictions. For controlled frequency-response comparisons in which every candidate should remain stable, use \(A\le2/7\); \(A=1/4\) is a convenient exact choice.
