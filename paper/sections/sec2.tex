\section{Ecological Model and Competing Memory Mechanisms}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Strong-Allee predator--prey backbone}
The nonlinear ecological model is
\begin{align}
 f_1(x,y) &= r x\left(1-\frac{x}{K}\right)\left(\frac{x}{A}-1\right)-\frac{axy}{1+hx},\\
 f_2(x,y) &= e\frac{axy}{1+hx}-my,
\end{align}
with locked parameters
\[
r=\frac32,\qquad K=1,\qquad a=1,\qquad h=\frac12,\qquad e=\frac45,\qquad m=\frac25.
\]
The coexistence equilibrium and Jacobian are
\[
x^*=\frac23,\qquad y^*(A)=\frac{2(2-3A)}{9A},
\]
\[
J(A)=\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{7A-2}{8A} & -\dfrac12\\[1ex]
\dfrac{2-3A}{10A} & 0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For a commensurate Caputo model, local stability follows the Matignon sector condition~\cite{F05}. In the complex-pair regime $2/7<A<10/19$, the critical order is
\[
\alpha^*(A)=\frac{2}{\pi}\arctan\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{4D(A)-T(A)^2}}{T(A)}\right),
\]
with $T(A)=\operatorname{tr}J$ and $D(A)=\det J$. At $A=0.30$, $\alpha^*\approx0.969012276$, so the fractional system with $\alpha=0.9$ is stable although the integer-order limit is unstable~\cite{P01}. For most discrimination experiments we use $A\le2/7$, where the common operating point remains in the stable regime.

\subsection{Four rival mechanisms}
Dimensional consistency is enforced with the reference time $\tau_0$~\cite{F02,F03,F04}. Around the operating point, the four candidate mechanisms are
\begin{align*}
\text{ODE:}&\quad \dot\xi=J\xi+Bu,\\
\text{Caputo:}&\quad \tau_0^{\alpha-1}{}^CD_t^\alpha\xi=J\xi+Bu,\\
\text{DDE:}&\quad \dot\xi=A_0\xi(t)+A_1\xi(t-\tau)+Bu,\\
\text{latent:}&\quad \dot q=\bar A q+\bar Bu,\qquad y=\bar Cq.
\end{align*}
Only the Caputo mechanism carries a fractional order. The finite latent class is restricted by an explicit state-dimension budget $m$.

\paragraph{Combined fractional-delay stress-test model.}
To address the interaction of memory and explicit delay directly, the numerical study also uses
\begin{align}
\tau_0^{\alpha-1}{}^CD_t^\alpha x(t)
&=P(x(t);A)-\frac{a x(t)y(t)}{1+h x(t)}+u(t),\\
\tau_0^{\alpha-1}{}^CD_t^\alpha y(t)
&=e\frac{a x(t-\tau)y(t)}{1+h x(t-\tau)}-m y(t).
\label{eq:fdmodel}
\end{align}
A constant equilibrium history is used on $[-\tau,0]$. Equation~\eqref{eq:fdmodel} reduces to the pure Caputo model at $\tau=0$ and to the retarded DDE benchmark at $\alpha=1$. It is used as a numerical stress test, not as an additional headline theorem.
