\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:T9b} (main paper)]
Split the integral (1) into three regions: \((0, \ell)\), \([\ell, L]\), and \((L, \infty)\).

\textbf{Low-rate tail} \((0, \ell)\): integrating the density over time,
\[
\int_0^T \int_0^\ell \lambda^{-\alpha} e^{-\lambda t} d\lambda dt
\le T \int_0^\ell \lambda^{-\alpha} d\lambda
= \frac{T \ell^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}.
\]

\textbf{High-rate tail} \((L, \infty)\): by Tonelli,
\[
\int_0^T \int_L^\infty \lambda^{-\alpha} e^{-\lambda t} d\lambda dt
= \int_L^\infty \lambda^{-\alpha-1}(1-e^{-\lambda T}) d\lambda
\le \frac{L^{-\alpha}}{\alpha}.
\]

\textbf{Middle interval} \([\ell, L]\): since \(\lambda^{-\alpha} e^{-\lambda t}\) is decreasing in \(\lambda\) for fixed \(t\), the gap between left and right Riemann sums is at most
\[
h \left( \ell^{-\alpha} e^{-\ell t} - L^{-\alpha} e^{-Lt} \right)
\le h \ell^{-\alpha}.
\]
Integrate over \(t \in [0,T]\), multiply by \(c_\alpha\), and sum the three contributions.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Explicit parameter choice for tolerance epsilon}

For a given tolerance \(\varepsilon\), it suffices to choose
\begin{align}
\ell &= \left( \frac{\varepsilon(1-\alpha)}{3 c_\alpha T} \right)^{1/(1-\alpha)}, \\
L &= \left( \frac{3 c_\alpha}{\alpha \varepsilon} \right)^{1/\alpha}, \\
h &\le \frac{\varepsilon \ell^\alpha}{3 c_\alpha T},
\qquad
m \ge \frac{L-\ell}{h}.
\end{align}

This construction is conservative, not complexity-optimal; it is sufficient for an existence theorem. In practice, faster convergence can be achieved with optimized Gauss--Laguerre or adaptive quadrature, but the construction above guarantees polynomial scaling.

\paragraph{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:T20} (testing bound under bounded safe excitation).}
By the definition of $\widehat E_m^{\rm IA}$ there is an explicit padded competitor $\widetilde k_m\in\mathcal K_m$ with $\|k_\alpha-\widetilde k_m\|_1\le\widehat E_m^{\rm IA}$.  For every $u\in\mathcal U_m$, Young's inequality gives
\[
\|(k_\alpha-\widetilde k_m)*u\|_2
\le \widehat E_m^{\rm IA}\,B_{\mathrm{eff}}.
\]
Hence the Gaussian KL divergence of this fixed two-point subproblem satisfies
\[
D_{\rm KL}(P_{C_\alpha}^u\|P_{\widetilde k_m}^u)
=\frac12\|R^{-1/2}S((k_\alpha-\widetilde k_m)*u)\|_2^2
\le U_m.
\]
The composite minimax risk is at least the Bayes risk of this particular two-point subproblem.  Pinsker's inequality and Bretagnolle--Huber therefore give the first two lower bounds~\cite{T01}; for common-covariance Gaussians the two-point Bayes error is exactly $\Phi(-\sqrt{D_{\rm KL}/2})$, yielding the third because this function decreases with KL.  Since the KL ceiling is uniform in $u\in\mathcal U_m$, taking the outer infimum over experiments preserves the bound.

\paragraph{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:T22} (hierarchy-uniform Strong-Allee budget).}
Upper bound: by linearity in the positive mode measure,
\[
d(k)\le\sum_j c_j F(\lambda_j)=\sum_j[c_j w(\lambda_j)]\,\frac{F(\lambda_j)}{w(\lambda_j)}
\le M_T\max_{\lambda}\frac{F(\lambda)}{w(\lambda)},
\]
because $\sum_j c_j w(\lambda_j)=\|k\|_{L^1(0,T)}\le M_T$.  Attainment: the single-mode kernel $k^*$ has mass exactly $M_T$, one rate in the window, and achieves $d(k^*)=M_T F(\lambda^*)/w(\lambda^*)$, so the supremum is a maximum and the stated value is exact.  One-sided safety $x^*+x_k(t)\ge A+\eta$ for all $k\in\mathcal K_m$ forces $c\,d(k)\le\rho_\eta$ for every $k$, hence $c\le\rho_\eta/d_{\mathrm{rob}}(\mathcal K_m,u_0)$, and multiplying by $\|u_0\|_2=1$ gives the bound.

\paragraph{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:polebranch} (pole/branch-cut decomposition).}
Deform the Bromwich contour for $(s^\alpha-\lambda)^{-1}$ onto a Hankel contour about the negative real axis, using the principal branch of $s^\alpha$. The sector condition gives exactly one principal-sheet zero $s_0=\lambda^{1/\alpha}$, with residue $(s_0/(\alpha\lambda))e^{s_0t}$. The jump across $s=re^{\pm i\pi}$ is
\[
\frac{1}{r^\alpha e^{-i\alpha\pi}-\lambda}-\frac{1}{r^\alpha e^{i\alpha\pi}-\lambda}
=\frac{2ir^\alpha\sin(\alpha\pi)}{r^{2\alpha}-2\lambda r^\alpha\cos(\alpha\pi)+\lambda^2},
\]
which yields the stated branch integral. The strict upper sector bound keeps the denominator nonzero for $r>0$; the small- and large-arc contributions vanish in the standard Hankel deformation for $t>0$.
