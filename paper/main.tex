\AIMSAuthors{Ibrahim Alraddadi$^{1,*}$}

\begin{AIMSAbstract}
A fitted fractional order $\alpha$ does not identify a fractional mechanism: on a finite horizon $[0,T]$, delayed feedback or a few hidden states reproduce similar trajectories. We ask when a \emph{safe} controlled experiment can separate these mechanisms in a strong-Allee predator--prey system with coexistence equilibrium $x^*=2/3$, $y^*(A)=2(2-3A)/(9A)$, whose model, locked parameters and Jacobian $J(A)$ are inherited from a previously submitted companion paper and are not claimed as new. Two obstructions are separated. The first is structural and is \emph{not} binding: the collocated Caputo transfer decays as $O(\omega^{-\alpha})$, which no finite rational latent transfer and no strictly proper retarded-delay model reproduces exactly. The second is quantitative and \emph{is} binding: positive exponential mixtures approximate the memory kernel in $L^1(0,T)$ at a root-exponential rate, so that $m(\varepsilon)=O(\log^2(1/\varepsilon))$ hidden states suffice; computed directly at the prey-response level, the $L^1$ surrogate error falls from $1.29$ at $m=4$ to $4.8\cdot10^{-5}$ at $m=128$ and the induced minimax testing error rises to $0.49999$, so at attainable latent orders the finite-state rival is indistinguishable. Against that obstruction we test whether safe excitation must sacrifice information. It must not. In a four-class BIC benchmark over three amplitudes ($2268$ cells, no divergences) reducing amplitude fails as a safety strategy --- PRBS still crosses the Allee threshold in $71\%$ of cells at half amplitude while remaining the most informative design --- and only one of six classical waveform families is safe, scoring macro-accuracy $0.514$ against $0.537$ overall. Searching $163\,840$ candidate waveforms outside those families under a hard margin constraint at the \emph{same} amplitude budget yields piecewise-constant designs that are both safe and far more informative: macro-accuracy $\mathbf{0.803}$ with zero threshold crossings, against $0.514$ for the only safe classical design and $0.763$ for the best classical design of any kind, which crosses in $35.7\%$ of cells. A posteriori validated integration certifies these trajectories, whereas an invariant-ball certificate admits only $\|u\|_\infty\le1.4\cdot10^{-3}$, $70\times$ smaller. The safety--informativeness trade-off reported for classical excitation families is therefore an artifact of the waveform parameterisation, not a property of the system; what survives is the latent-complexity obstruction, since even the best safe design recovers only $0.475$ recall on the finite-latent rival. Safe identification of ecological memory is limited by the declared rival-complexity budget $m$, not by the safety margin.
\end{AIMSAbstract}
