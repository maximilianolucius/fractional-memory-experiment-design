\begin{abstract}
This paper develops a safety-constrained framework for actively discriminating fractional (Caputo), delayed (DDE), and bounded-complexity latent-state memory mechanisms around a certified strong-Allee predator--prey equilibrium. We derive exact linear transfer-function separation on collocated channels, a finite-horizon exponential-approximation obstruction, and computable input and observation designs. A calibrated, stable-only simulation benchmark with information-criterion model selection attains a macro-averaged accuracy of $0.54$ (chance $0.25$) and reveals a safety--informativeness trade-off: transient designs stay within the certified safe set but discriminate weakly, whereas broadband designs discriminate better yet cross the Allee threshold. The principal limitation is that safe active discrimination of subtle memory is intrinsically hard; a full Bayesian sequential-design layer is left to future work~\cite{P01}.
\end{abstract}
