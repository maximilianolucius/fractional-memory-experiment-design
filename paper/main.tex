\begin{abstract}
This paper develops a safety-constrained framework for discriminating Caputo, delayed, and finite-state latent memory mechanisms around a strong-Allee predator--prey equilibrium. We derive exact transfer-function separation, finite-horizon exponential approximation bounds, optimal input/observation designs, and a calibrated simulation benchmark. At the kernel level, a bounded-complexity latent hierarchy yields an explicit testing obstruction under a protocol-restricted Strong-Allee safety budget. We then address the ecological response directly, avoiding a false factorization of the fractional resolvent. On the prey-to-prey channel, the exact Caputo impulse response decomposes into a principal-sheet pole pair, realized exactly by a stable two-state integer-order block, plus a branch-cut Laplace mixture. Interval arithmetic gives finite-state $L^1(0,T)$ approximation enclosures; at $\alpha=0.85$, $T=12$, and $A=0.25$, the enclosure falls from $0.5335$ at $m=4$ to $0.0070$ at $m=32$. Combined with the declared pulse peak cap and Gaussian observation model, these enclosures imply non-vacuous minimax testing-error lower bounds approaching $1/2$ as latent complexity increases. The full-response result is linearized and prey-channel specific; a hierarchy-wide Strong-Allee safety closure for physically constrained full-state latent rivals remains open. The benchmark attains macro-accuracy $0.54$ versus chance $0.25$ and exposes a safety--informativeness trade-off: transient inputs cross the Allee threshold less often but discriminate weakly, whereas broadband inputs are more informative but less safe. A Bayesian sequential-design layer is specified but not executed.
\end{abstract}
