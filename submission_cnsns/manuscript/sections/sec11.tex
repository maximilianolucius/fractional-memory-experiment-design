\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The question posed at the outset --- what would it take, experimentally, to establish that an ecological system carries fractional memory --- receives a quantitative answer with one part negative and one part constructive.

The constructive part is that the apparent conflict between safety and information is largely an artifact of how the perturbation is parameterised. Across the six classical excitation families the picture is discouraging: the accuracy-leading designs leave the coexistence basin, and the single family that never does is the least informative of the six. Reducing amplitude does not resolve this, and the realised response gain shows why --- once a trajectory escapes, its excursion is set by the basin geometry rather than by the input, so the linear certificate cannot license safety at any of the amplitudes tested. Optimising the waveform \emph{shape} instead, over a piecewise-constant class the classical families do not contain and at the same peak-amplitude budget, recovers most of what the safe classical design loses: four-class accuracy $0.803$ with no observed threshold crossings, against $0.514$. Safety of these trajectories is verified a posteriori, and rigorously enclosed for four of the eight headline cases. A Pareto cost survives --- the best unsafe candidate still separates the hypotheses better --- so the correct statement is not that safety is free, but that the severe frontier previously read off the classical families is not a property of the ecology.

The negative part is independent of input design and does not yield to it. Computed directly at the prey-response level rather than inferred from the memory kernel, finite-state surrogates reach an $L^1$ error of $5\cdot10^{-5}$ at latent order $128$, and the exact two-point minimax floor they induce reaches $0.49999$ under the declared protocol --- uniformly over every admissible input, since the surrogate error is an operator norm. Structural distinctness (Theorem~\ref{thm:T4}) therefore coexists with practical indistinguishability at attainable latent orders (Corollary~\ref{cor:closure}). The benchmark is consistent with this, though there the model-selection penalty and the approximation-theoretic obstruction act in the same direction and are not separated, so the obstruction is asserted on the analytical grounds alone.

The practical consequence for experimental design is a change of question. Not \emph{which model fits best}, nor even \emph{which safe perturbation separates the mechanisms} --- that one now has a constructive answer --- but \emph{what latent complexity the experiment is willing to admit as a rival}. The allowed latent dimension, delay range, forcing bandwidth and observation horizon belong in the scientific specification of the experiment alongside organism and protocol, because it is that declaration, and not the safety margin, that bounds what can be identified.
